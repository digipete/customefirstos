# Start with the outcome

Understand the problem before choosing the technology.

## Why this matters

Technology choices made before the outcome is understood narrow thinking and turn assumptions into commitments.

## What this means in practice

Write the customer and service outcome in plain language. Name the decision before comparing tools. Trace how a technical choice could help or hinder the outcome.

## Questions to ask

- What outcome must change?
- Whose experience or work must improve?
- What decision do we actually need to make?

## During a Test & Learn Experiment

Test whether the proposed mechanism changes the outcome, not merely whether the technology functions.

## Watch out for

- starting with a preferred product
- treating delivery of technology as the outcome

## Related guidance

Use this principle with the CustomerFirst Decision Framework, Test and Learn guidance and evidence standard.
